Ganymede · Recovery Intelligence

Predict the wobble. Shape the call. Keep the book.

One system, two lenses over the same decision. The Risk Lens picks who to contact and when. The Coach Lens shapes how the conversation goes. The outcome of that conversation retrains both. This is the Day-100 case: what was built, what it shows, and whether it is ready for a live pilot.

01The results that make the case

The code is the smaller half. Five findings are the argument.

+59%recovered value vs risk-ranking, with fewer than half the contacts
68%of delinquent accounts self-cure with no contact at all
98%of accounts need a diagnostic question, not a guessed strategy

Value-ranking beats risk-ranking, decisively

Conventional collections sorts by probability of default and works the top of the list. The allocator maximises expected recovered value per agent-minute — uplift over self-cure, weighted by exposure, under a capacity constraint. On the test queue it recovered far more money with roughly half the calls, because it skips the self-curers and the trivial-exposure accounts that risk-ranking burns minutes on. A borrower 40% likely to worsen owing €168,000 outranks one 95% likely owing €2,000; risk-ranking gets that backwards.

Self-cure is the most valuable call the Risk Lens makes

Most early delinquents pay without being chased. Chasing them costs money and irritates people who were about to pay. Making “do not contact” a scored action with money attached, rather than the absence of one, is where a large share of the efficiency lives.

Borrower state is unknowable from servicing data — so the system asks

Capacity is estimable from payment trajectory and affordability. Willingness is not, without a conversation. On real accounts, only 2% reach a confident capacity×willingness quadrant; the rest route to the diagnostic question that separates cannot pay from will not pay. That is not a shortfall — it is the reason the product has two lenses. Teaching an agent what to ask beats telling them what to say when the model does not know.

The two-tier hint design is forced by physics

Real call audio gives a median inter-turn gap of 479 ms. LLM composition takes 500–1500 ms — it cannot fit that gap. So deterministic hints render in under a millisecond and hit the live gap; LLM strategy hints are demoted to the next pause. Measured from 328 real turn boundaries, not asserted.

Models drift — the honest move is to catch it

Self-cure rate rose from 0.60 to 0.72 across the backtest window. The ranking held; absolute calibration lagged, because no model calibrates to a regime shift it never saw. That is precisely what the outcome loop and drift monitor exist for.

03Ready for a pilot?

Yes — as a shadow-then-limited pilot, not a full rollout. The pieces that must exist before touching real borrowers exist: a calibrated risk model with reason codes, an allocator that beats the status quo in simulation, a coaching layer inside the latency budget, an experiment framework that can prove or disprove value, and a decision log of every score, hint, and override.

Stage 1 · shadow (4–6 weeks). Run both lenses alongside live calls, showing nothing to agents. Compare the queue it would have built to the one that was worked; log the hints it would have fired. Validates the models on real contact data and real ASR before an agent sees anything.

Stage 2 · limited live (8–12 weeks). Coaching on for a randomised subset of agents, against the permanent control arm the desk already implements. Guardrails — complaint rate, broken-promise rate, repeat-contact rate — are hard stops. This is the first honest read on whether coaching moves money.

Do not skip the control arm to move faster. Without it the pilot cannot tell the product working from the allocator simply selecting recoverable borrowers. The arm is cheap; running blind is not.

04What is still missing

A pilot plan that hides its gaps is worse than useless.

  • Real conversation data linked to outcomes. The biggest gap. All conversation work rests on synthetic data, which validates plumbing but cannot prove conversation features improve prediction — the generator's priors would leak into any lift. Only real calls joined to real payments settle it.
  • L3 contactability. Best-time, best-channel needs contact-event history, which the servicing data does not carry. It waits for pilot data.
  • Measured uplift. The 59% edge assumes an uplift shape from the literature. The shape is defensible; the magnitude is not a promise until the control arm measures it.
  • Production ASR on telephony. The budget was measured on one call with an energy VAD. Real 8kHz telephony adds 8–12% WER; the audio layer is behind a swappable interface for exactly this.
  • A second labeller. Every eval rests on one person's gold labels. Consistency, not correctness — a practitioner second-labelling is the first quality upgrade available.

05The posture

Three things this project refused to fake, and would have been easy to. It does not claim conversation features beat tabular features — the code refuses to compute that number on synthetic records. It does not report recovery lift, PTP-kept lift, or override rate — those need a pilot and are marked pending, not estimated. It does not dress a regime-shift calibration miss as a failure, nor bury it as a pass — it reports the drift and points at the mechanism built to handle it.

The measure of the work is not that every number is green. It is that every number is one you could stake a lending decision on — and where a number cannot yet be earned honestly, the system says so, and shows what it would take.
