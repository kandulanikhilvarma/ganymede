Ganymede · Day-100 case

Predict the wobble. Shape the call. Keep the book.

One system, two lenses over the same decision. The Risk Lens picks who to contact and when. The Coach Lens shapes how the conversation goes. The outcome of that conversation retrains both. This is what was built, what it shows, and whether it is ready to touch a real borrower.

01The results that make the case

The code is the smaller half. These findings are the argument. Every figure is regenerated from the pipeline on each build and carries the kind of evidence behind it — hover a badge for its source.

Value-ranking beats risk-ranking, decisively

Conventional collections sorts by probability of default and works the top of the list. The allocator maximises expected recovered value per agent-minute — uplift over self-cure, weighted by exposure, under a capacity constraint. On the test queue it recovered far more money with roughly half the calls, because it skips the self-curers and the trivial-exposure accounts that risk-ranking burns minutes on. A borrower 40% likely to worsen owing €168,000 outranks one 95% likely owing €2,000; risk-ranking gets that backwards.

The edge is not a constant. It is largest when capacity is scarce and shrinks as capacity approaches full coverage — which is the honest shape of the result and the reason the allocator studio ships the whole frontier rather than one flattering point.

Self-cure is the most valuable call the Risk Lens makes

Most early delinquents pay without being chased. Chasing them costs money and irritates people who were about to pay. Making “do not contact” a scored action with money attached, rather than the absence of one, is where a large share of the efficiency lives.

Borrower state is unknowable from servicing data — so the system asks

Capacity is estimable from payment trajectory and affordability. Willingness is not, without a conversation. On real accounts only — reach a confident capacity×willingness quadrant; the rest route to the diagnostic question that separates cannot pay from will not pay. That is not a shortfall — it is the reason the product has two lenses. Teaching an agent what to ask beats telling them what to say when the model does not know.

Median confidence across the queue is —. Willingness is capped low by construction here, because the servicing data carries no contact events, no promises, and no channel response to learn it from.

The two-tier hint design is forced by physics

Real call audio gives a median inter-turn gap of —, measured across — real turn boundaries. LLM composition takes 500–1500 ms — it cannot fit that gap. So deterministic hints render in under a millisecond and hit the live gap; LLM strategy hints are demoted to the next pause.

03Ready for a pilot?

Yes — as a shadow-then-limited pilot, not a full rollout. The pieces that must exist before touching real borrowers exist: a calibrated risk model with reason codes, an allocator that beats the status quo in simulation, a coaching layer inside the latency budget, an experiment framework that can prove or disprove value, and a decision log of every score, hint, and override.

Stage 1 · shadow (4–6 weeks). Run both lenses alongside live calls, showing nothing to agents. Compare the queue it would have built to the one that was worked; log the hints it would have fired. This validates the models on real contact data and real ASR before an agent sees anything.

Stage 2 · limited live (8–12 weeks). Coaching on for a randomised subset of agents, against the permanent control arm the desk already implements. Guardrails — complaint rate, broken-promise rate, repeat-contact rate — are hard stops, not report lines. This is the first honest read on whether coaching moves money.

Do not skip the control arm to move faster. Without it the pilot cannot tell the product working from the allocator simply selecting recoverable borrowers. The arm is cheap; running blind is not.

Every logged decision carries its arm and its propensity, non-optionally. A retrain aborts if either is absent, which is what stops the models quietly learning the allocator's preferences instead of borrower behaviour.

04What is still missing

A pilot plan that hides its gaps is worse than useless.

  • Real conversation data linked to outcomes. The biggest gap. All conversation work rests on synthetic data, which validates plumbing but cannot prove conversation features improve prediction — the generator's own priors would leak into any lift. Only real calls joined to real payments settle it, and evals/metrics.py refuses to compute the number until then.
  • L3 contactability. Best-time, best-channel needs contact-event history, which the servicing data does not carry. It waits for pilot data.
  • Measured uplift. The allocator's edge assumes an uplift shape from the literature. The shape is defensible; the magnitude is not a promise until the control arm measures it — which is why it is badged simulated and not backtested.
  • Production ASR on telephony. The budget was measured on one call with an energy VAD at 16 kHz. Real 8 kHz telephony adds an estimated 8–12% word error rate; the audio layer sits behind a swappable interface for exactly this.
  • A second labeller. Every eval rests on one person's gold labels. That buys consistency, not correctness — a practitioner second-labelling is the first quality upgrade available.
  • Outcome support for the playbook. Every coaching strategy is currently seeded from collections practice with a support count of zero. None is promoted until real outcomes back it above the threshold, and the desk shows the count on every hint so thin evidence looks thin.

05The posture

Three things this project refused to fake, and would have been easy to. It does not claim conversation features beat tabular features — the code refuses to compute that number on synthetic records. It does not report recovery lift, PTP-kept lift, or override rate — those need a pilot and are marked pending, not estimated. It does not dress a regime-shift calibration miss as a failure, nor bury it as a pass — it reports the drift and points at the mechanism built to handle it.

The measure of the work is not that every number is green. It is that every number is one you could stake a lending decision on — and where a number cannot yet be earned honestly, the system says so, and shows what it would take.
